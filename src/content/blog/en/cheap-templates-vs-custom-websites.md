As a studio that builds custom websites, the answer you'd expect from us is "custom is always better". That isn't honest. There are plenty of situations where a template is the right decision, and pushing custom just burns a client's money.

This article tries to draw the line as clearly as we can.

## When a template is the right call

### You're validating an idea

If you don't yet know whether the business will work, spending IDR 25 million on a website is unnecessary risk. A IDR 2 million template that's live next week gets you the answer faster.

### The requirement is genuinely standard

A restaurant with a menu, opening hours, and a reservation button. A salon with a service list and bookings. If thousands of businesses have an identical requirement, a template probably already does it well.

### The budget is genuinely tight

A template site that's live today beats a custom site that's been "under consideration" for two years. An imperfect online presence is far better than none.

### The site isn't a core part of the business

If your customers come from referrals and the website mainly exists to verify you're real, a template covers it.

## When a template gets expensive

Templates save at the start, then invoice you later in the following forms.

### When you start fighting the structure

Every template is built on assumptions. While your needs match them, everything is smooth. The moment you need something the author didn't anticipate, modification costs climb fast — and the result is often fragile.

We've spent time modifying templates that, recalculated afterwards, would have been cheaper to rebuild from scratch.

### When performance holds you back

Commercial templates are usually built to serve many cases at once. That means they ship code for features you don't use: sliders, portfolio filters, animations, and dozens of options.

The result is a heavy page. For a site depending on search traffic and mobile visitors, that's a direct business cost. How to measure it is in [the Core Web Vitals article](/blog/en/speed-up-your-website-core-web-vitals/).

### When you look identical to your competitors

Popular templates are used by thousands of businesses. In industries where trust drives the purchase — professional services, property, healthcare — looking generic has a real cost.

### When the workflow doesn't fit

This is what happens most often to a growing business. The template handles "submit a contact form". Your business needs "guest picks dates, system checks availability, takes a deposit, assigns staff". No template does your specific flow.

## Hidden costs on both sides

**Template:**
- Extra plugin licences to cover the gaps
- Time lost modifying things that fight the structure
- Migration when you eventually have to move
- Performance that's never genuinely good

**Custom:**
- Longer build time before anything exists
- Dependence on a developer for structural changes
- Maintenance cost that must be budgeted
- Higher risk when scope isn't clear from the start

## The middle path that usually makes most sense

For many businesses, this sequence is more rational than picking either extreme:

1. **Start with a template or a simple landing page.** Live in two weeks.
2. **Collect data for three to six months.** Which pages get read, which questions repeat, where leads come from.
3. **Build custom based on that data**, not on guesses.

A custom site built after you understand visitor behaviour is almost always better than one built up front on assumptions.

## The three questions that decide it

If you have to choose today, answer these:

**1. Is your business workflow standard or specific?**
Standard → template. Specific → custom.

**2. Is the website your main revenue channel or a supporting one?**
Supporting → template. Main → custom.

**3. Do you already know what you need, or are you still guessing?**
Still guessing → template first. You know → custom.

If two of three point to a template, take the template. You can always upgrade later — and you'll upgrade with far better information.

## What can't be compromised, either way

Template or custom, these have to be true:

- The domain is registered in your name
- You have full hosting access
- The site opens quickly on a phone
- The technical SEO foundation is correct
- The contact path is clear and works
- Backups exist and have been tested

If those six aren't in place, the low price is irrelevant. The full vendor evaluation checklist is in [choosing a web development vendor](/blog/en/checklist-for-choosing-a-web-development-vendor/).
